# Mobile apps (Android + iOS): what changes for the integrations

> Researched 2026-10-07 by independent agents quoting official pages. **Single-source, not adversarially verified, never run by a human.** The mobile approach (Capacitor / TWA / PWA / native) was NOT known when this was written: where the answer depends on it, the item says `depends_on_approach`. Read the project chat "Web app mobile development" and reconcile.

## GOOGLE_PLAY: Google Play Developer Policy Center rules for a wrapped Bmapz AI web app (payments, account deletion, user data, webview/TWA, target API, new-account testing, developer verification, sign-in)

- **PAYMENTS: Play Billing is mandatory for any Play-distributed app that requires or accepts payment for in-app features or services. 'Cloud software and services (such as ... business productivity software ...)' is listed explicitly as an example that needs Play Billing, so a B2B SaaS subscription is squarely inside the rule.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Do NOT sell or upgrade starter/growth/scale/enterprise plans (or any add-ons or credits) inside the Android app. Either stay consumption-only (see next findings) or integrate Play Billing (15%-ish fees; not researched here). The consumption-only route is the realistic one.
  
  > Play-distributed apps requiring or accepting payment for access to in-app features or services, including any app functionality, digital content or goods (collectively “in-app purchases”), must use Google Play’s billing system for those transactions unless Section 3, 8, or 9 applies. ... cloud software and services (such as data storage services, business productivity software, and financial management software).
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738
- **PAYMENTS: I found NO B2B / organisation / enterprise exemption. The complete list of 'must not use Play Billing' cases is physical goods, physical services, credit-card/utility bill remittances, peer-to-peer payments, online auctions, tax-exempt donations, gambling, and Payments Center prohibited categories; the FAQ adds clinical services, insurance/stock/tax services, gift cards, loyalty points, 1:1 live services, and digital goods that can only be consumed outside any Play app. None covers SaaS bought by businesses. The FAQ also says category does not matter.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Do not argue 'we are B2B so we are exempt' to Google. Plan on the consumption-only route. (The only enterprise-flavoured exemptions on other Play policies are for 'permanently private' internal-distribution apps, which Bmapz is not.)
  
  > Does your billing policy change depending on my app category? No. All app categories are subject to Google Play's Payments policy.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818
- **PAYMENTS: The exemption that actually fits Bmapz is 'consumption-only' (reader-style): users log in and use a service that was bought elsewhere (your web Stripe Checkout). The catch: nothing at all can be purchasable inside the app.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Ship the Android app as login-and-use only. Remove every purchase surface from the Android build: plan upgrades, seat add-ons, AI-credit top-ups, one-off purchases. Keep subscriptions sold only on ai.bmapz.com via Stripe.
  
  > Can I offer a consumption-only (reader) app on Google Play? Yes. Google Play allows any app to be consumption-only, even if it is part of a paid service. For example, a user could log in when the app opens and access content paid for somewhere else. Remember, consumption-only means that any product(s) or service(s), whether digital or physical, cannot be purchased from within the app.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818
- **PAYMENTS (the exception to the exception): even a consumption-only app may not lead users to another payment method via links, buttons, webviews, messaging, CTAs, listing text, or sign-up flows. A wrapped web app that still shows your Stripe Checkout, pricing page, 'Upgrade' buttons or Stripe Customer Portal inside the WebView/TWA violates this. Allowed: links to administrative info (account management page, privacy policy, help centre) as long as that page does not eventually lead to an alternate payment method; and, for consumption-only apps, plain-text mentions of purchasing options WITHOUT direct links.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** In the Android build (detect Capacitor/TWA user agent or a ?app=android flag) hide or neutralise: Stripe Checkout, Customer Portal link, pricing/upgrade pages and CTAs, trial-expired paywall buttons, and any 'Add card' step in sign-up. Replace with neutral text (e.g. 'Your plan is managed by your account admin'). Be conservative: text that urges buying outside the app is only explicitly blessed for consumption-only apps and only without links. Do not deep-link from the app to /pricing or the Stripe Portal. Provide the web sign-up and billing only outside the app (email, website).
  
  > Other than the conditions described in Section 3, Section 8, and Section 9, apps may not lead users to a payment method other than Google Play's billing system. This prohibition includes, but is not limited to, leading users to other payment methods via: ... In-app webviews, buttons, links, messaging, advertisements, or other calls to action; and In-app user interface flows, including account creation or sign-up flows ... Also: 'For services and products that are consumption only ..., developers may choose to provide additional information about purchasing options without direct links, including using language like: "Go to our website to upgrade your subscription to Premium"'. Also: 'Developers can refer users to administrative information – like an account management page, privacy policy, or to a help center – as long as the webpage does not eventually lead to an alternate payment method prohibited by the Payments policy.'
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818
- **PAYMENTS, US ONLY: because of the Epic v. Google injunction (effective 2025-10-29) Google currently does not require Play Billing for US users on phones/tablets and allows link-outs and alternative billing, but only via enrolled programs (launched 2025-12-09) with fees and reporting. A revised 'Modified Injunction' based on a 2026-03-04 settlement had been requested; the page does not say it was entered. Google reserves the right to end the programs. Fees (alternative billing table): auto-renewing subscriptions 10%; other digital items 20% (new installs) / 25% (existing installs); first USD 1M annual earnings 10%. Reporting/payment of fees started 2026-10-01 (ECL download reporting deadline moved to 2026-12-01). The External Content Links program needs enrolment/approval and integration of Google's external-content-links APIs (information screen) - that implies native Android code.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `depends_on_country` (confidence high). **Action:** Treat this as optional and temporary. Do NOT build on it for launch. Only revisit if US in-app conversion becomes important; it would require Play Console enrolment, API integration (not feasible in a pure TWA/PWA; inference), a customer-support/refund process, and the fee/reporting obligations. Restrict any US link-outs to US users only. The rule that applies everywhere else is unchanged.
  
  > Google will not require the use of Google Play Billing in apps distributed on the Google Play Store, or prohibit the use of in-app payment methods other than Google Play Billing. ... On March 4, 2026, we entered a new settlement agreement with Epic and the parties have asked the US District Court to enter a revised Modified Injunction. ... Our current policies and programs remain in effect. Developers may continue to link users to external content or to offer alternative billing systems by complying with the following policies and programs (as launched on December 9, 2025)
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165
- **PAYMENTS, OTHER REGIONS: alternative billing / link-out programs exist for India, South Korea (alternative billing alongside Play, 4 percentage points off the service fee) and the EEA (DMA programs). I found no program for Brazil or most other countries. Separately, Play Billing's requirement does not apply in a country where Play Billing has not launched.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `depends_on_country` (confidence medium). **Action:** Assume the standard rule (consumption-only, no purchase links) for all users outside the US/EEA/India/South Korea. If the business is in Brazil, nothing special applies; Brazilian users are under the normal rule.
  
  > Developers selling digital content or services can now offer their users in the EEA an alternative to Google Play's billing system within their apps, subject to program requirements. ... we will offer all developers the ability to offer an alternative billing system alongside Google Play's billing system for users in India and South Korea ... the service fee will be reduced by 4%.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818
- **ACCOUNT DELETION: Any app that lets users create an account in the app must (a) give an in-app path to request deletion of the account and associated data, and (b) give a web link resource where users can request deletion, entered in the Data safety/App content form in Play Console. The in-app path can be satisfied by an in-app link that takes the user to your web deletion resource. Exempt only: permanently private apps and enterprise device-management apps.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Add a visible 'Delete account' item in Settings in the Android build (it may open the web deletion flow), and enter https://ai.bmapz.com/DataDeletion in Play Console's Data deletion field. Fill in the Data deletion questions in the Data safety form.
  
  > All developers must complete new Data deletion questions in the Data safety form on the App content page in Play Console. ... provide users with an in-app path to delete their app accounts and associated data; and provide a web link resource where users can request app account deletion and associated data deletion. ... As an alternative, you can choose to provide a link within your app that takes users to your app account deletion web resource. ... If your app offers account creation in any part of the app experience, then you still need to offer app account deletion even if some features can be accessed without an account.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111
- **ACCOUNT DELETION - WEB PAGE QUALITY: the web link must load, make the deletion pathway prominent, name the app or developer exactly as in the Play listing, and let the user request deletion WITHOUT reinstalling or opening the app. A form or support email is acceptable. If steps are needed first (e.g. cancelling a subscription), say so clearly and offer a support flow. Deactivation/freezing does not count. Data may be retained for security, fraud prevention or regulatory reasons if disclosed in the privacy policy. All data you declare as collected is in scope; you should also ask service providers to delete.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Audit https://ai.bmapz.com/DataDeletion: it must be publicly reachable without login (it is a SPA route; test with JS-light fetch/Google reviewer), mention 'Bmapz AI' exactly as the Play listing and developer name, offer a form or email that works for users who cannot log in, explain the Stripe-subscription cancellation step, and state retention (e.g. invoices) and timing. Make the backend delete per-user rows AND the company-scoped OAuth tokens belonging to that user, and trigger deletion at Supabase/Stripe/other processors. Decide and document what happens to company (tenant) data when the last admin deletes their account.
  
  > The weblink must be functional (for example, loads without error), relevant in scope (for example, the pathway to request account deletion should be prominently featured and easily discoverable on the page) and reference the app or developer name (that is, as it appears on your store listing in Google Play). ... your web resource should give users a way to request that their data be deleted without sending the user back to the app and requiring them to re-download it to submit their request. ... Temporary account deactivation, disabling, or “freezing” the app account does not qualify as account deletion.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111
- **DATA SAFETY FORM: mandatory for every app, must match the privacy policy, and must declare data collected via libraries/SDKs AND via a WebView that your app opens if you control the code delivered through it (exempt only when users browse the open web). Wrapping ai.bmapz.com therefore means declaring everything the web app collects. Data types to expect: email address, name/user IDs, emails, calendar events, files and docs (Drive), photos and videos, other in-app messages (WhatsApp/social), contacts, purchase history, app activity, diagnostics. Transfers to a 'service provider' acting on your instructions are treated separately from 'sharing'. Google does not verify the form; you are responsible for accuracy.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Prepare a data inventory (what Bmapz and its processors receive from Gmail, Calendar, Drive, YouTube, Analytics, Search Console, Ads, Meta/WhatsApp, LinkedIn, X, TikTok, Canva) and answer the form from it: collected = yes for many types; encrypted in transit = yes; users can request deletion = yes (with the web link). Keep it in sync with /PrivacyPolicy.
  
  > "Collect" means transmitting data from your app off a user’s device. ... Webview: It also includes user data collected from a webview which has been opened from your app, if your app is in control of the code/behavior delivered through that webview. You do not need to declare data collection from a webview in which users are navigating the open web. ... You alone are responsible for making complete and accurate declarations in your app’s store listing on Google Play.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10787469
- **PRIVACY POLICY: required in the Play Console field AND inside the app; must disclose access, collection, use, sharing of all user and device data; must name the developer/company or the app; must be a public, non-geofenced, non-PDF, non-editable URL; every app needs one. Personal and sensitive data (explicitly including 'authentication information' and financial/payment information) must be limited to purposes the user reasonably expects, handled securely with modern cryptography, and never sold.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Link https://ai.bmapz.com/PrivacyPolicy in Play Console and inside the app (Settings/login screen). Verify it is reachable without login, not geofenced, labelled 'Privacy Policy', names 'Bmapz AI' / the legal entity as in the Play listing, covers third-party OAuth tokens, per-company token storage, AI/LLM subprocessors, retention and deletion. Encrypt tokens at rest (best practice; Play only requires secure handling and HTTPS) and never sell the data.
  
  > All apps must post a privacy policy link in the designated field within Play Console, and a privacy policy link or text within the app itself. ... The entity (for example, developer, company) named in the app's Google Play store listing must appear in the privacy policy or the app must be named in the privacy policy. ... Please make sure your privacy policy is available on an active, publicly accessible and non-geofenced URL (no PDFs) and is non-editable. ... Handle all personal and sensitive user data securely, including transmitting it using modern cryptography (for example, over HTTPS). ... Not sell personal and sensitive user data.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311
- **PROMINENT DISCLOSURE & CONSENT: if data access is outside what a user would reasonably expect (the policy's own example is collection 'in the background when the user is not engaging with your app'), you need an in-app disclosure (not only in the privacy policy) shown in normal use, followed by affirmative consent. Bmapz's scheduled/automated reading of connected Gmail, Calendar, Drive and social accounts is arguably in this category (my interpretation). Also, July 15 2026 clarification: User Data requirements apply to third-party AI integrations, including limited use, disclosure and consent.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence medium). **Action:** Before each 'Connect' OAuth step in the app, show a plain-language screen: what is read (e.g. Gmail messages), what the AI does with it, who processes it (named AI providers), and require an explicit tap to continue. Do not treat back/dismiss as consent. List AI subprocessors in the privacy policy.
  
  > Prominent disclosure: You must provide an in-app disclosure of your data access, collection, use, and sharing. The in-app disclosure: Must be within the app itself, not only in the app description or on a website; ... Cannot only be placed in a privacy policy or terms of service. ... We're clarifying that our User Data requirements also apply to third-party AI integrations and that developers remain responsible for ensuring compliance with this policy, including limited use, disclosure and consent.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10144311
- **GOOGLE API SERVICES USER DATA POLICY (applies to Gmail/Drive/Calendar data regardless of Play): 'Limited Use' - use data only for user-facing features visible in the app, no transfers except narrow cases, no humans reading data without consent/security/legal reasons, no ads, no selling, no credit/lending use. Restricted/sensitive scopes may require an annual third-party security assessment and Letter of Assessment, depending on the API and the number of user grants. This is a Google (not Play) requirement, but Play reviewers and Google OAuth review both apply it.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `yes` (confidence high). **Action:** Keep the Limited Use commitments in the privacy policy and in engineering (no ad use, no human review of Gmail content, no sale). Confirm separately which Gmail/Drive scopes are restricted and whether the annual security assessment is needed; this affects the calendar for public Gmail access more than Play does.

- **GOOGLE OAUTH IN EMBEDDED WEBVIEWS IS PROHIBITED: Google OAuth requests must not be sent to an embedded user-agent under the developer's control. This hits the Capacitor/WebView approach directly: both 'Continue with Google' and every Google integration OAuth popup must run in the system browser/Custom Tab (or native Google sign-in), not inside the app WebView. A TWA/PWA runs in the browser engine itself, so it is not an embedded user-agent (my inference from how TWA works; test it).**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `depends_on_approach` (confidence high). **Action:** If Capacitor/WebView: open Google authorization URLs with an external browser (Custom Tab, e.g. Capacitor Browser plugin) and return to the app via a verified Android App Link or custom scheme; do not rely on window.open popups. If TWA/PWA: test popup/redirect flows end to end on a real Android phone. Also verify Supabase's Google sign-in flow behaves the same way.
  
  > A developer must not direct a Google OAuth 2.0 authorization request to an embedded user-agent under the developer's control. Embedded user-agents include, but are not limited to, software libraries that allow a developer to insert arbitrary scripts, alter the default routing of a request to the Google OAuth server, or access session cookies.
  
  Source: https://developers.google.com/identity/protocols/oauth2/policies
- **WEBVIEW / MINIMUM FUNCTIONALITY: Play bans apps whose primary purpose is a webview of a website only WITHOUT the website owner's permission; Bmapz owns ai.bmapz.com, so the webview-spam rule is not a blocker. A separate quality rule rejects apps lacking 'the basic degree of adequate utility as mobile apps'. A thin wrapper is therefore allowed in principle, but a reviewer may judge it low-utility.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `depends_on_approach` (confidence high). **Action:** Wrapper is acceptable for Google. To lower rejection risk add a few genuinely mobile features (push notifications, deep links, share target, biometric lock, offline error screen) and make sure the first screen is functional, never a blank/loading page. Be ready to state that the website is yours.

Apps that crash, do not have the basic degree of adequate utility as mobile apps, lack engaging content, or exhibit other behavior that is not consistent with a functional and engaging user experience are not allowed on Google Play.
  
  Source: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034
- **TRUSTED WEB ACTIVITY (TWA) EXPECTATIONS: TWA opens your PWA from an Android app, Chrome 72+, trust is proven with Digital Asset Links (app and site from the same developer), content should meet the Add-to-Home-Screen (PWA installability) criteria, and the host app cannot read web cookies/localStorage. The Android docs page I fetched says nothing about Play Billing, OAuth or sign-in inside TWA, so those points are unconfirmed.**  
  Applies to Bmapz: `depends_on_approach` (confidence medium). **Action:** If TWA: publish /.well-known/assetlinks.json on ai.bmapz.com (Cloudflare Pages) with the Play app-signing SHA-256 fingerprint, add a valid web manifest + service worker, and run the Lighthouse installability audit. Trusted Web activities need to meet the same Add to Home Screen requirements. ... The host app doesn't have direct access to web content in a Trusted Web Activity or any other kind of web state, like cookies and localStorage.

- **NEW 2027 SIGN-IN REQUIREMENT (ZERO-TAP RESTORE): from April 2027 apps that support sign-in (optional or mandatory) on phones/tablets must restore the signed-in state when a user moves to a new Android device via device-to-device transfer or cloud backup, using the Android Restore Credentials API (Credential Manager, Android 9+). **Impact on the integrations plan:**
- Redirect URIs: for the confidential-client code exchange the provider redirect URIs stay as they are (https on the API host, soon https://api.bmapz.com). No provider needs a new custom-scheme redirect IF the API's final hop redirects the system browser to an app deep link after finishing the exchange. - Popup flow must change for native: window.open + postMessage popups do not work reliably in a WKWebView wrapper, and Google forbids embedded user-agents. - Sign-in: choose between (A) hide Continue with Google on iOS only and keep email+password (zero Apple extra work; add in-app password-reset/set-password for Google-only users), or (B) add Sign in with Apple.
